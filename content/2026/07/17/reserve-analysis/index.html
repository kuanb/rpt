Rose Park Terrace Reserve Study Comparison

Barrera & Company report created September 10, 2025 (planning period beginning 2026) versus Association Reserves report dated June 19, 2026 (planning period beginning 2027).

Overall assessment: The 2026 study is a materially different physical and financial model, not a routine update. It raises the total current replacement-cost inventory, extends or resets many component schedules, lowers the accrued fully funded balance, and recommends a more targeted long-term funding path.

Executive conclusions

  • The new study is not simply an inflation update. It re-architects the inventory: 62 one-to-one component matches, 11 split/merged groups, nine newly funded component groups, and two prior funded groups removed from reserve funding.
  • Total current replacement cost rises 7.9%, but the fully funded balance falls 2.2%. The apparent contradiction is caused by longer useful lives, reset ages, and re-scoped components. The building is estimated to cost more to replace, but less of that cost is treated as already accrued deterioration.
  • The reserve position is materially weaker. The starting reserve balance falls from $140,522 to $105,940 and percent funded falls from 14.0% to 10.5%. The new firm classifies the special-assessment/deferred-maintenance risk as high.
  • Useful lives generally stayed the same or became longer. Among 62 direct matches, 43 kept the same useful life, 17 became longer, and only two became shorter. Relative to a normal one-year roll-forward, 30 of 62 direct matches were pushed later, 29 stayed on schedule, and three moved earlier.
  • Several large remaining-life resets are supported by recent work, but several are not documented. Supported resets include the 2026 metal awnings, 2025 water heater, 2023 fire alarm, and 2025 balcony inspection. The lobby tile (1 to 39 years) and manager furnace (6 to 26 years) should be confirmed with invoices or installation records.
  • Near-term funding rises, while the long-term funding curve becomes much less aggressive. The new recommendation is $121,296 for 2027, 12.2% above the prior first-year contribution. The old recommended walk-forward then escalated 10% every year and reached 237% funded by 2055; the new plan targets approximately 100% by 2055.
  • The 30-year expense comparison is assumption-sensitive. Over the common 2027-2055 period, the new report projects 18.7% less nominal spending even though its current-cost inventory is higher, mainly because it uses 3.5% inflation instead of 5.0% and changes project timing/scope.
  • The 27-versus-28 unit count must be resolved. It affects per-unit contribution figures and is a basic factual inconsistency between the reports.

5. Funding and 30-year projections

The funding plans are not directly comparable without recognizing their different objectives. The 2025 recommended walk-forward starts at $108,119 and compounds contributions by 10% annually. The 2026 plan starts at $121,296, increases about 12% annually through 2032, then approximately 2.45% annually, targeting about 100% funded by 2055.

  • 2025 recommended plan reaches 100% funded in 2044 and 237% by 2055.
  • 2026 recommended plan reaches 100.3% in 2055.
  • Over 2027-2055, projected contributions total $17.68 million under the old walk-forward versus $7.64 million under the new plan. The difference reflects funding policy, not only component costs.
  • Over the same years, nominal projected expenditures total $6.20 million in the old study and $5.05 million in the new study, 18.7% lower.
  • Both studies place the largest expenditure spike in 2050: $1.92 million in the old study versus $1.19 million in the new study.
The new study produces lower nominal 30-year expense totals despite a higher current-cost inventory because inflation and project timing differ.
The 2025 recommended walk-forward compounds at 10% indefinitely; the 2026 plan sharply front-loads funding and then moderates increases.
The old walk-forward materially overshoots 100% funding; the new plan converges toward 100%.

  7. Building exterior repairs: Confirm that $36,200 adequately replaces the prior $54,198 combined stucco and wood-trim repair allowances.
  8. Roof consultant/PM: Confirm that the new $15,000 allowance adequately replaces the prior $29,906 combined design/specification and management allowances.

Appendix B: Method and limitations

This comparison reconciles components by physical function rather than by exact label. Splits and merges are evaluated on an aggregate-cost basis. Useful-life statistics are limited to one-to-one matches because a single lifespan comparison is not meaningful for multi-component splits or merges. Current costs are nominal estimates in each report; the projection comparison is affected by different inflation, interest, start-year, component-scope, and funding assumptions.
